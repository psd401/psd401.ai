The Playbook

Everything your team touches, in one place. Three pieces of paper and four weekly questions — that’s the entire system. (Just want the steps? The Get-Started flyer is the whole thing on one page.)

The one rule: one build per team per cycle. If your idea won’t fit in six weeks, it’s two builds. Split it.

Good problems live inside the goals you already have — a smoother MTSS huddle, scaffolds built faster so the bar stays at grade level, family communication that actually lands. You’re not hunting for something shiny. You’re clearing the path to goals you’ve had all along.

The cycle at a glance

Week What happens
1 — Plan Pick one problem. Write your one-page Build Plan. Lock it by Friday and post your first Weekly Check-In.
2–5 — Do Build it and use it. Adjust as evidence comes in. Post a Weekly Check-In every week, same day each week.
6 — Study & Share Your AI Agent drafts the Wrap-Up from your check-ins. Team edits (~20 minutes), makes the call — keep it, stop it, or run it again — and publishes. Read the other teams’ Wrap-Ups.
7–8 — Between cycles Teams breathe. Cabinet reads everything, the superintendent sets the next Quarterly Focus, and the next cycle is teed up.

Your team

Three to six people: a team lead (owns the build), a scribe (posts the check-in), two to four doers, and a sponsor from cabinet who clears blockers when you’re stuck. Willingness matters more than title.

Paper #1 — The Build Plan (start of cycle)

One page. Written fast — it’s a starting point, not a contract. Ask your AI Agent to help you draft it.

Team: ___ Cycle: ___ Date: ___

The problem. Two or three sentences. What’s eating time, causing friction, or not working — specifically, in your building or department?

Our build. “We’re going to build , because we think it will .”

How we’ll know. Two or three signs of success. At least one you can count. And name the thing that must NOT get worse (“…and families still feel personally communicated with”).

Week-one plan. Just the first week. You’ll adjust the rest as you learn — that’s the point.

Not this cycle. What you’re deliberately NOT taking on. Your polite way of keeping the build small enough to finish.

What it could become. If this works, what do we hand everyone else? Usually: a skill added to the PSD AI Agent, or a tool in AI Studio. One sentence is plenty — you’ll know more by week 6.

Paper #2 — The Weekly Check-In (every week)

Four questions, two or three sentences each, posted by your scribe in your team’s chat space — same day every week. Five minutes, tops. Your AI Agent can remind you and post the scaffold.

Meetings are fine — the space is the memory, not the meeting room. Our pilot teams taught us this: don’t force every conversation into chat. Meet in person, talk in the hallway, hop on a call. Then capture it — a photo of the whiteboard, a transcript or recording, three bullets of notes — dropped into the space. Rule of thumb: if it’s not in the space, the system can’t learn from it.

Tried — What did we actually do this week? Name real moves, not intentions. Learned — What did we find out? Include the surprises; they’re the valuable part. Stuck — Where are we blocked? Be honest, not heroic. Needs — What would unblock us, and from whom? Be specific.

Skip a week and the system goes blind to your team. Miss two in a row and someone will check on you — that’s care, not surveillance.

Paper #3 — The Wrap-Up (end of cycle)

Your AI Agent drafts this from your own check-ins. Your team edits it in about twenty minutes and makes the call.

What we tried — from your Build Plan.

What happened — what you actually did and what resulted, in plain terms.

What we learned — including the surprises and the things you still don’t understand.

The call — pick one: - KEEP IT. It worked. Now we make it real for everyone — usually by adding it as a skill in the PSD AI Agent or building it as a tool in AI Studio — and here’s what other teams should know before borrowing it. - STOP IT. It didn’t work. Here’s why, and what nobody else should waste a cycle repeating. (Publishing this is a badge of honor here — stopped builds teach the most.) - RUN IT AGAIN. Not sure yet — but now we have a sharper question. Here’s the question the next cycle starts from. (Two cycles without a sharper question usually means it’s really a Stop.)

What you do NOT have to do

  • Be a programmer. If you can describe your problem clearly, you can build with your Agent. That’s the whole point.
  • Solve the whole problem. You have to learn something, not fix everything.
  • Defend a stopped build. Stopping openly is the model working.
  • Hold every conversation in the chat space. Meet however you like — just drop the artifacts (photos, notes, transcripts) into the space afterward.
  • Coordinate with other teams. The agent digest surfaces overlaps for you.
  • Make anything pretty. Substance over polish, every time.

Treat the cycle seriously — and lightly. The point is to learn, not to perform.
